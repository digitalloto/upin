# Carrying a balloon beacon on the drone: the numbers

Generated by `tools/compare_balloon_carry.py` from labelled physics (`upin/simulation/balloon_physics.py`). These decide between designs; none of it is a measurement.

## Assumptions

- helium net lift 1.05 kg/m³ at sea level, 15 °C; sphere drag coefficient 0.47 (falls at high Reynolds number: drags below could be 2–4× smaller); air density 1.225 kg/m³
- envelope 0.06 kg/m²; a 200-bar cylinder 1.2 kg per litre of water capacity
- drone 2.0 kg, thrust-to-mass 22.5 m/s² (hover at 44% throttle); beacon payload 0.3 kg; balloon ascent 5.0 m/s
- the simulator's jammer: 18 dB inside its radius, 6 dB less per doubling of distance; a balloon's GNSS works again below 10 dB
- unknown wind sigma 5.0 m/s while the balloon climbs

## 1. The balloon a beacon needs

To lift 0.3 kg and still climb at 5.0 m/s: diameter **1.61 m**, volume 2.18 m³, envelope 0.49 kg, helium 0.389 kg. (Radiosonde practice is 1.2–1.5 m at launch; the model's drag coefficient is conservative.)

## 2. Carrying it inflated

| Speed | Drag of the balloon | As a fraction of hover thrust | Of maximum thrust |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 m/s | 14.6 N | 75% | 32% |
| 10 m/s | 58.4 N | 298% | 130% |
| 15 m/s | 131.5 N | 671% | 292% |

Holding position in wind with the balloon attached, keeping 20% thrust in reserve: about **5 m/s** of wind at most. The balloon also fouls the propellers and must be kept clear of them.

## 3. Carrying it deflated

Gas 0.389 kg in a 10.88 L cylinder weighing about 13.06 kg, plus the envelope 0.49 kg, parachute and beacon 0.3 kg: roughly **13.9 kg** carried in steel (a composite cylinder about half that), inflation in flight taking minutes. A cubic metre of gas at 200 bar is a 5-litre cylinder: scuba-sized, not pocket-sized.

## 4. Released inside a jammer: when does it become useful?

Released at the jammer's radius (1000 m), the balloon's own GNSS works again at about **2.5 km** up, **8 minutes** later. Until then its position is a dead-reckoned guess: wind unknown gives a sigma of about **2.5 km** at that moment. After it, the beacon knows itself to a few metres, but the drone has been without it for those minutes.

## 5. The balloon-lifted drone

To carry a 2.0 kg drone the balloon is about **1.7 m** across. Drag at 5 / 10 m/s: 16 / 63 N against the 20 N of hover thrust it replaces. Holding position in a 5 m/s wind takes 35% of the drone's maximum thrust. Good for loitering in calm air; not for a mission with transit.

## 6. A tethered aerostat over the base

- 300 m tether, 3 m aerostat with 60 N net lift, wind 3 m/s: leans 17°, offset 88 m downwind at 287 m up; a 2 m/s wind uncertainty moves it ±92 m. Survey the anchor; measure the tether angle or track it from two sites to tighten that.
- 300 m tether, 3 m aerostat with 60 N net lift, wind 8 m/s: leans 65°, offset 272 m downwind at 126 m up; a 2 m/s wind uncertainty moves it ±28 m. Survey the anchor; measure the tether angle or track it from two sites to tighten that.

## Comparison

| Option | Beacon knows itself to | Useful after | Mass on the drone | Coverage | Cost in flight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Launch-site balloon (layer 151) | ~5 m (own GNSS above the jammer) | before the mission | none | tens of km from 20–30 km up, 1.5–2 h per balloon | none |
| Carried balloon, released when jammed | ~2.5 km for 8 min, then ~5 m | 8 min after release | ~13.9 kg deflated, or unflyable inflated | grows as it climbs | heavy; drag if inflated |
| Breadcrumbs (layer 152) | 2–20 m (the fix at release + fall drift) | immediately | tens of grams each | 1–3 km from the trail, line of sight | a dispenser |
| Tethered aerostat over the base | 10–50 m (blowdown) | persistent | none | a few km round the base | none |

**Verdict from these numbers:** drop breadcrumbs on the way in; fly launch-site balloons for area coverage; keep an aerostat over the base; do not carry balloons on the mission drone.
